\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Work-related accidents and diseases kill nearly three million people a year and cost roughly 4\% of global GDP \cite{ilo2023}. Personal protective equipment (PPE) is often the last barrier between a worker and a hazard, and checking that it is worn usually falls to a Health, Safety, and Environment (HSE) operator watching camera monitors. Vigilance in such tasks declines within 20 to 35 minutes \cite{parasuraman1984}, and operators at diamond plants caught only about half of the targeted risk behaviors, with many false alarms \cite{donald2015}.

Automated PPE detection, mostly with YOLO-family detectors \cite{ultralytics2023,ppeyolo2024,edgeyolo2024}, could take over part of this work. Yet most systems are built and evaluated on construction imagery, and few of the studies we reviewed (Section~\ref{sec:related}) report results at an operating chemical plant. Public datasets (Roboflow PPE-Detection \cite{roboflowppe}, SH17 \cite{ahmad2024sh17}) focus on helmets and vests. They contain little chemical-specific PPE such as gas masks and goggles, and few scenes with dust, harsh light or uniforms that resemble PPE. When site imagery is added to training, its benefit is usually confounded with the larger amount of data.

We study both gaps at the phosphoric-acid unit of OCP Group's Maroc Chimie complex in Safi, Morocco. Our method is supervised fine-tuning of YOLOv8n on labeled data from several sources. It is not domain adaptation in the sense of learning from unlabeled target data. We contribute a dataset and an evaluation design:
\begin{itemize}
  \item 207 annotated frames from 14 recordings at an operating phosphoric-acid unit, split by recording into five folds so that no recording appears in both training and test;
  \item four models trained with one recipe, each differing from the previous one in a single factor, which separates the effect of plant imagery from that of training-set size;
  \item a documented protocol (thresholds, classes, checkpoint, and recording-level confidence intervals for every number), with a discussion of what detection accuracy does not tell us about safety.
\end{itemize}
