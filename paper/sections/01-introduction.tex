\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Work-related accidents and diseases kill nearly three million people yearly, injure 395 million more, and cost roughly 4\% of global GDP \cite{ilo2023}. Personal protective equipment (PPE) is often industry's last hazard barrier, but a mandate is only as good as its enforcement: typically a Health, Safety, and Environment (HSE) operator watching a wall of IP camera monitors.

That fails because vigilance depletes rapidly: detection performance collapses within 20--35 minutes of continuous monitoring \cite{parasuraman1984}, and diamond-plant operators caught only about half of targeted risk behaviors, with high false-alarm rates \cite{donald2015}. A camera yields 525{,}600 minutes of footage per year for roughly one minute of accident; surveillance only works if attention aligns with that exact minute.

Automated PPE detection, dominated by YOLO-family detectors \cite{ultralytics2023,ppeyolo2024,edgeyolo2024}, could remove this bottleneck, but published systems are built and evaluated on construction-centric benchmarks; to our knowledge none has been evaluated on a real, operating chemical plant. Two gaps explain why: training data (Roboflow PPE-Detection \cite{roboflowppe}, SH17 \cite{ahmad2024sh17}) revolves around helmets and vests, under-representing chemical-specific classes (gas masks, goggles) and conditions (dust, harsh lighting, PPE-like uniforms); and few systems adapt to an individual site at all, exactly where generic detectors break down. This paper's central experiment measures that breakdown directly.

We address these gaps with an IP-camera-retrofit PPE compliance system developed for and evaluated at OCP Group, Morocco. The core claim is domain-specific, not architectural: a detector adapted for industrial/chemical conditions outperforms one trained only on the standard construction benchmark on chemical-plant imagery. Contributions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Real-plant central experiment: a controlled comparison of a generic detector \cite{roboflowppe} against our industrial-adapted model on held-out OCP imagery and an industrial proxy set. The comparison, not a new architecture, is the claim.
  \item Composite domain-adapted dataset: Roboflow PPE-Detection \cite{roboflowppe}, SH17 \cite{ahmad2024sh17}, the Roboflow gas masks corpus \cite{roboflowgasmasks}, and a self-annotated, weighted OCP corpus.
  \item Retrofit deployment model: runs on existing camera/network infrastructure; alerts reach channels safety staff already use.
\end{itemize}
