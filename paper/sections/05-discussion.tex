\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{What the comparison shows.} At equal training-set size, plant imagery raises mAP@50 on unseen recordings by 42.3 points, while more public data adds 2.9. The gain comes from site PPE that the public data do not contain, such as work jackets that no public model reads as vests, and helmets and boots seen small and from a distance. Adding public industrial imagery even drops plant accuracy below the construction-only benchmark, so more data from another setting is no substitute for site data. Over the construction-only benchmark the total gain is 28.6 points. Three controls make this number attributable: test recordings never seen in training, a benchmark trained and scored with the same recipe, and training sets of equal size.

\textbf{Limitations.} The plant data cover one unit on one day (12 of the 14 recordings within 13 minutes), and the only people in view are the authors, so the range of worker appearance, seasons and plant areas is narrow. Test recordings show the same people as training recordings, and A may partly have learned their clothing; the gain on new staff may be smaller. SH17 is subsampled at random rather than by class, which thins its rarest classes. Within our compute budget we tested one detector size (YOLOv8n) at one input size (640). At that size goggles, gloves and gas masks are too small to detect reliably, so the system cannot yet check them.
