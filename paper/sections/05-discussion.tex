\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

Generic benchmark training does not transfer to an operating chemical plant: a construction-benchmark model and an industrially adapted model deliver different quantities on the same plant frames, and that difference is the contribution. Public PPE corpora favor construction imagery (near-frontal, daylight, helmets/vests \cite{roboflowppe,ppeyolo2024}) while a phosphate site adds dust, oblique angles, and gas masks/goggles; the composite dataset closes that gap by fusing sources \cite{ahmad2024sh17}, adding the gas-mask corpus \cite{roboflowgasmasks}, and weighting OCP fine-tuning toward the deployment site. Limitations: SH17 subsampling is unstratified, thinning scarce classes and validation support (Section~\ref{sec:methods-dataset}); no\_goggle has zero instances from any source, so its 0.0\% row is data absence, not failure; small, occluded objects (gloves, goggles) are the nano detector's weakest case; camera blind spots are inherited, not corrected; and the generic baseline trains on far less data, so the comparison measures deployment yield rather than an isolated domain-coverage effect.
