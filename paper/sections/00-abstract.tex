Work-related accidents and diseases kill nearly three million people each year, and camera monitoring still depends on operators whose vigilance declines within 20 to 35 minutes. Detectors for personal protective equipment (PPE) are trained and tested almost only on construction imagery. We ask whether plant imagery helps a lightweight detector at an operating phosphoric-acid unit of OCP Group (Safi, Morocco), and whether any gain comes from the plant imagery itself or from having more training data. We fine-tune YOLOv8n with one recipe on four training sets, each changing one factor: construction images, all public sources, public sources enlarged to our training-set size, and public sources plus 207 annotated plant frames. Plant frames are split by recording into five folds, so no recording appears in both training and test, and a single protocol scores every model with recording-level bootstrap confidence intervals. On held-out plant recordings, the construction-only benchmark reaches 43.9\,$\pm$\,1.0\% mAP@50 on its five classes and the model trained with plant frames 72.5\%. At equal training-set size, plant imagery adds 42.3 points (95\% CI 37.8 to 47.2) and extra public data 2.9. Public industrial images without plant frames score below the benchmark, because the plant's PPE looks different from public PPE. Gloves and goggles are still missed at plant camera distances. Inference takes 11.7\,ms per frame on a T4 GPU (batch 1). We measure detection accuracy; compliance and accident reduction were not measured.
