Industrial accidents kill nearly three million workers each year, yet installed IP camera networks still rely on human observers whose attention collapses within 20--35 minutes. Automated personal protective equipment (PPE) detection could help, but published systems are trained and evaluated only on construction-centric benchmarks, never on real, operating chemical-plant imagery. We present a PPE compliance pipeline evaluated at an OCP Group (Office Ch\'{e}rifien des Phosphates) phosphate-processing plant in Safi, Morocco: a YOLOv8n detector fine-tuned on a composite dataset (public construction and industrial-safety corpora, a gas-mask set, and weighted OCP imagery) covering the gas masks, camera angles, and site uniforms construction benchmarks omit. Against a detector trained only on the construction benchmark, the adapted model reaches 65.5\% mAP@50 on held-out OCP imagery versus 18.5\% for the generic baseline (49.6\% vs.\ 0.4\% on an industrial proxy set), showing the gain is domain-specific, not architectural. The pipeline retrofits onto existing cameras with no new hardware.
