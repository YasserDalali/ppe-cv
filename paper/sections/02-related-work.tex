\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

Most PPE detectors now use YOLO \cite{ultralytics2023,ppeyolo2024,edgeyolo2024} and are usually trained on Roboflow PPE-Detection \cite{roboflowppe}; SH17 \cite{ahmad2024sh17} extends the label space to 17 industrial classes (Table~\ref{tab:relwork}). Class lists center on helmets and vests, and respiratory and eye protection is largely missing. Site conditions are rarely targeted, and few of the studies we reviewed report evaluation at an operating chemical plant.

The evaluation protocol affects reported accuracy as much as the model does. Frames from one video are near-duplicates, so a frame-level split tests a detector on scenes it has already seen; splitting by recording or camera avoids this. A gain from adding site data can be credited to the site only if training-set size is held constant. None of the studies we reviewed does both on operating-plant imagery. Vigilance studies \cite{parasuraman1984,donald2015} motivate automation, and they also show that false alarms shift the burden to whoever reviews the output.
