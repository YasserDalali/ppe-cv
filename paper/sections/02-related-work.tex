\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

PPE detection has consolidated around YOLO \cite{ultralytics2023,ppeyolo2024,edgeyolo2024}, mostly trained on Roboflow PPE-Detection \cite{roboflowppe}. The shared limitation (Table~\ref{tab:relwork}): class inventories center on hard hats and vests, respiratory/eye protection is absent, nothing adapts to site dust/lighting/uniforms, and none has been evaluated on a real operating chemical plant.

SH17 \cite{ahmad2024sh17} widens the label space to seventeen classes (the broadest public industrial inventory) but stays generic-manufacturing, with no domain-shift mechanism for a specific plant. It is an ingredient for a site-adapted model, not a substitute.

A separate literature motivates detection at all: vigilance decays within the first half hour regardless of instruction \cite{parasuraman1984}, and diamond-plant operators caught only half of targeted risk behaviors with high false alarms \cite{donald2015}, which shift the vigilance burden downstream to whoever reviews the output.

No prior work reports a controlled comparison of generic vs.\ industrially adapted detection on operating-plant imagery (Table~\ref{tab:relwork}); that comparison is this paper's center.
