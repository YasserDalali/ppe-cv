One constraint shapes the design: retrofit onto installed IP cameras, ruling out new hardware and favoring a small detector.

\subsection{Dataset Construction}
\label{sec:methods-dataset}

No single public corpus covers a chemical site's class mix, so training fuses three public sources plus one site-specific corpus under one class map (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}): Roboflow PPE-Detection \cite{roboflowppe} (helmet/vest construction imagery), SH17 \cite{ahmad2024sh17} (17 classes, industrial scenes), and Roboflow gas masks \cite{roboflowgasmasks} (384 images). Unified ten-class map: Person, helmet, vest, gloves, boots, goggles, no\_helmet, no\_gloves, no\_goggle, gas mask. Explicit negative classes let the detector localize \emph{absence} directly rather than infer it from a missing detection.

Fusing annotations is not file concatenation: each source's class IDs index its own ontology (SH17's 0--16 is not the fused map), so naive merging reinterprets in-range IDs as the wrong class and discards out-of-range images as corrupt. A preliminary naive merge rejected roughly 43\% of training images this way. We remap explicitly first: each SH17 class maps to its fused ID or is excluded (body parts), recorded, labels rewritten before training.

SH17 outnumbers the other three combined by an order of magnitude (8{,}000 vs.\ roughly 1{,}000: 308 Roboflow PPE-Detection, 361 gas-mask, 352 OCP), and both remap and per-epoch cost scale with pool size. Since well-represented classes saturate with a few hundred examples, we cap SH17 at 1{,}500 training images, 300 each for validation/test, uniform random with a fixed seed, bounding compute on a single Colab GPU at no material cost to common-class accuracy, at the price of thinning scarce SH17 classes and val/test metrics on a subsample (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

Gas masks come from the Roboflow gas masks corpus \cite{roboflowgasmasks}, safety-critical yet under-represented in construction data; unlike OCP, not oversampled (a generic stand-in, not the deployment domain). The fourth component, self-annotated OCP site-camera imagery, captures phosphate dust, lighting transitions, and site uniforms, annotated against the fused map and given higher sampling weight; a held-out, disjoint portion forms the chemical-plant evaluation set (Section~\ref{sec:methods-comparison}), subject to imagery clearance. The fused dataset splits 70\%/15\%/15\% independently per source (unstratified), fixed seed.

\subsection{Preprocessing}
\label{sec:methods-preprocessing}

Images are resized to $640\times640$. Augmentation targets IP-camera failure modes: HSV jitter (dust haze/glare), horizontal flipping, random erasing (occlusion), and mosaic (scale/context), disabled in the final epochs for realistic late-training frames.

\subsection{Model Training}
\label{sec:methods-training}

The detector is YOLOv8n \cite{ultralytics2023}: 3.0M parameters, 8.1 GFLOPs at $640\times640$, 6.3 MB, small by design since inference must run on commodity hardware alongside existing video infrastructure. Training: Google Colab, up to 50 epochs, $640\times640$, batch 16, patience 20 (the reported run trained the full 50 epochs); auto-configured SGD, LR 0.01, momentum 0.937, weight decay $5\times10^{-4}$, 3-epoch warmup. Best checkpoint by validation fitness exported as \texttt{best.pt}, used throughout.

\subsection{Generic-vs-Adapted Comparison Protocol}
\label{sec:methods-comparison}

The central experiment: does generic benchmark training transfer to an operating chemical plant? The \emph{generic baseline} trains solely on Roboflow PPE-Detection \cite{roboflowppe} (308 images, 9 classes; 58.1\% precision, 64.4\% recall, 59.1\% mAP@50 on its own validation split), the off-the-shelf public model. The \emph{industrial-adapted model} is the fused-dataset detector of Section~\ref{sec:methods-training}.

Both are evaluated on two held-out sets: OCP site imagery (Section~\ref{sec:methods-dataset}, the deployment domain) and an \emph{industrial proxy} of SH17 and gas-mask images \cite{roboflowgasmasks}, publishable without clearance. Metrics use classes common to both maps (person, helmet, vest, gloves, negative states) at identical resolution/thresholds. The baseline also trains on far less data, a confound discussed in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}; the comparison measures what the public model actually delivers on plant imagery.
